# Community events inclusion (broader festivals & markets)

> **Puddles includes events that give little ones something meaningful to do — not simply events where children are welcome.**

## 1. Inclusion guideline

For broader community events, require at least **one strong toddler-relevant reason to go**, ideally two.

### Strong signals

- Dedicated kids area / Kids Zone
- Crafts, sensory play, chalk, bubbles, face painting
- Music, dancing, puppets, magic
- Animals, vehicles, trains, farm activities
- Play structures / inflatables appropriate for young kids
- Cultural activities children can participate in
- Seasonal experiences: pumpkins, holiday activities, parades
- Short, casual, drop-in-friendly performances

### Supporting signals

- Daytime
- Free or low cost
- Stroller-friendly
- Food available
- Outdoor / room to move
- Easy to stay for only 30–90 minutes

### Usually exclude

- “All ages” with no actual child activity
- Primarily food/drink/shopping
- Adult concerts where kids are merely permitted
- Late-night festivals
- Activities mainly for 6+
- Farmers markets with only vendors and produce

### Farmers markets

> **The market itself is not an event. A special kids activation happening at the market can be.**

- “Mountain View Farmers Market” → no
- “Zucchini Car Race at Mountain View Farmers Market” → yes

## 2. Activity type: Festivals & Community

Do **not** add **Family Friendly** as an Activity Type. That describes suitability, not what the event is.

### Definition

Broader local events that are not designed specifically for young children, but offer a clear, meaningful experience for little ones and families.

The event should give children something to **explore, watch, participate in, or experience**—not simply allow kids to attend.

Typical examples:

- Street festivals with a Kids Zone
- Cultural festivals with family activities
- Community celebrations
- Seasonal fairs
- Open houses with interactive experiences
- Neighborhood events
- Special market activations
- Public safety or vehicle events
- Community days with crafts, music, performances, or play

### Include when

Use **Festivals & Community** when:

- There is a clear reason a child ages 0–5 would enjoy the event.
- The event includes kid-friendly activities such as crafts, music, dancing, face painting, animals, vehicles, performances, play areas, or demonstrations.
- Little ones can actively explore, watch, participate, or engage with the experience.
- Families can enjoy the event casually without committing to a long structured program.
- The event works as a realistic short outing with a young child.
- The event does not fit more naturally into a more specific Puddles activity type.

### Do not include when

Do not use **Festivals & Community** when:

- Children are technically welcome, but there is no meaningful experience for them.
- The event is primarily focused on food, alcohol, shopping, networking, or adult entertainment.
- The only family-friendly attribute is that strollers or children are allowed.
- Programming is mainly designed for older children or adults.
- A more specific activity type better describes the experience.

#### Examples

**Include**

- Street festival with a dedicated Kids Zone
- Cultural festival with music, dancing, crafts, and family activities
- Fire station open house with trucks and demonstrations
- Community fair with face painting, games, and performances
- Farmers market hosting a special kids concert or activity
- Seasonal community celebration with pumpkin decorating or holiday activities

**Not Festivals & Community**

- Regular farmers market with vendors only
- Art & wine festival with no child-specific activities
- Adult concert that happens to allow children
- Food festival with no family programming
- Shopping event marketed as “all ages”

### Classification rule

> **Use Festivals & Community when a broader community event gives little ones something meaningful to do or experience—not simply because kids are welcome.**

If the event has a clear primary activity such as **Arts & Crafts**, **Music & Movement**, or **Parent & Me**, use that more specific activity type instead.

Keep **one primary Activity Type** per event. More specific types still win when accurate:

- Outdoor children’s concert at a festival → **Music & Movement**
- Pumpkin decorating → **Arts & Crafts**
- General festival with kids zone + food + music → **Festivals & Community**

Do **not** add a separate Festival filter — **Festivals & Community** is part of the Activity Type filter.

## 3. Behind-the-scenes tags (not visible filters yet)

Capture metadata for recommendations and copy later:

**Audience:** Little ones · All ages · Older-kid leaning

**Experience:** Kids Zone · Live music · Crafts · Food · Animals · Seasonal · Cultural · Outdoor

**Practical:** Free · Stroller-friendly · Drop-in · RSVP recommended · Worth a little drive

## 4. Filters

- Do **not** add a **Family Friendly** filter (everything on Puddles should already be family-relevant).
- Do **not** add a separate Festival filter.
- **Festivals & Community** is available in the Activity Type filter.

## 5. Card copy: explain why it’s on Puddles

For adult-leaning festivals, show the toddler-relevant reason immediately:

**Mountain View Art & Wine Festival**  
*Kids Zone with face painting, trampolines & more*

Or in Good to know:

**Why little ones might like it**  
Kids Zone with face painting, rides and activities.

## 6. Age guidance for broader events

Toddler-specific programming can still say **Best for ages 2–5**.

For broader festivals, avoid pretending they are designed for toddlers:

**All ages · Little ones welcome**

or:

**All ages**  
*Best for little ones who enjoy music, food & exploring.*
